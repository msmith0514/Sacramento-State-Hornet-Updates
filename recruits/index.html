PLAYER DEVELOPMENT

THE TECHNOLOGY BEHIND HORNET DEVELOPMENT

Simulation · Capture · Analysis · Transfer

Sacramento State's development model is built around a feedback loop: identify the opponent, goal or skill focus; create a challenging game-like rep; capture what happened; analyze the data and video; then use that information to choose the next drill, cue or individual plan.

Opponent, goal or skill focus

02CHALLENGE

Machine, bullpen, cage or live AB

03TEST

04ANALYZE

05ADJUST

HITTER DEVELOPMENT

GAME-LIKE SWINGS WITH OBJECTIVE FEEDBACK

The hitting stack combines pitch simulation, high-volume machine work, swing sensors, tracking data and video so hitters can train timing, pitch recognition, swing decisions and movement quality under increasingly game-like conditions.

PITCH SIMULATION

iPitch Pitching Machines

Tablet-controlled pitch simulation gives hitters repeatable exposure to the speed, movement and sequencing they are preparing to face. Coaches can build custom pitch libraries, program specific sequences or randomize pitches so the hitter cannot simply sit on a known machine pattern.

  • Opponent preparation: recreate pitch shapes, velocities, locations and sequences associated with a scheduled pitcher.
  • Pitch recognition: mix fastballs and breaking balls with uncertainty so the hitter must identify shape rather than anticipate the next setting.
  • Repeatable testing: return to the same pitch/location to evaluate whether an adjustment actually improved contact quality or decision making.
50-100 MPH0-3000 RPMProgrammed + random sequences
HIGH-VOLUME REPS

Mini Hack Attacks

The compact three-wheel machine is used when the goal is volume without sacrificing useful pitch shape. It is suited to cage stations, rapid rotations and targeted work in which hitters need many quality repetitions in a short period.

  • Fast station work: quick adjustments let coaches move through velocity, breaking-ball and zone-specific blocks efficiently.
  • Timing development: placing the machine closer can create a shorter reaction window while preserving a visible three-wheel release.
  • Defensive transfer: the same machine can be used for fungo-style work, allowing one tool to support both offensive and defensive development periods.
70+ MPH45 ft can simulate 90+ MPH250 ft fungo
VARSITY GAME SPEED

Senior Hack Attack

The full-size Hack Attack is the heavier game-speed machine in the hitting environment. Independent wheel controls allow coaches to create velocity and right- or left-handed breaking shapes while preserving a visible feed, acceleration and release cue.

  • Velocity exposure: train swing decisions and contact timing against high-end machine velocity.
  • Breaking-ball recognition: create curves, sliders and split-style shapes rather than relying only on straight fastball work.
  • Zone command: elevation and inside/outside adjustments let a coach attack specific areas of the strike zone without rebuilding the station.
100+ MPH53 in release point380 ft fungo
SWING SENSOR

Blast Motion

A sensor mounted to the bat knob provides immediate swing feedback and lets hitters connect what a swing felt like with what the bat actually did. The value is not a single number; it is the ability to track consistent swing checkpoints across drills and training phases.

  • Bat speed: monitor how efficiently the hitter is producing speed without losing control.
  • Attack angle and swing path: connect movement changes to the type of contact the hitter is trying to create.
  • Time to contact: evaluate how quickly the barrel gets organized when reaction time is reduced.
  • Video overlay: auto-clipped swings with metrics let coaches review movement and data together rather than in separate silos.

PITCHER DEVELOPMENT

MEASURE THE PITCH, SEE THE DELIVERY, BUILD THE PLAN

Pitching development uses ball-tracking, multi-angle video and scouting analytics to connect pitch design and command with actual game results. The same language can follow a pitcher from bullpen work to live at-bats and competition.

GAME-DAY TRACKING

Stadium TrackMan

The stadium installation captures objective ball-flight and location information from competition. Because the data comes from real games, coaches can compare what is trained in bullpens with how pitches actually behave when the result matters.

  • Release and flight: evaluate how the ball leaves the hand and how it moves through the zone.
  • Strike-zone information: review command, miss patterns and how pitch locations change by count or hitter side.
  • Game-to-practice transfer: use competition data to decide what a pitcher should address in the next bullpen, catch play or pitch-design session.
  • Context: pairing tracking information with video helps separate a one-pitch result from a repeatable movement or execution pattern.
Radar + visionRelease / flight / zoneLive game data
PORTABLE TRACKING

TrackMan Portable

The portable B1 unit brings the same objective feedback language into bullpens, cages, live at-bats and field sessions. It supports pitch-only, hit-only and live-at-bat modes, so a development session can be designed around the exact question a coach and player are trying to answer.

  • Pitch design: monitor velocity, spin and movement while testing grips or intent.
  • Command work: pair shape with location instead of treating velocity and strike throwing as separate goals.
  • Live AB evaluation: measure pitches and batted balls in the same competitive rep.
  • Consistent language: compare portable practice readings with stadium game information to see whether bullpen gains transfer.
Release speedSpin rateMovementLive-at-bat mode
MECHANICAL VIDEO

Seven Camera Angles

Seven synchronized viewpoints allow coaches and players to slow down the same rep from multiple perspectives. That matters because mechanical changes can look very different from open side, glove side, behind the pitcher, center-field or close-up views.

  • Pitchers: compare direction, posture, arm path, release and lower-half sequencing without relying only on what the delivery felt like.
  • Hitters: review load, move, barrel path, posture and contact position from the views that best expose each checkpoint.
  • Shared language: side-by-side and slow-motion review turns a verbal cue into something the athlete can actually see.
  • Data pairing: place video beside TrackMan or Blast metrics so a number has a visible movement explanation.
7 viewsSlow motionSide-by-side review
ANALYTICS + SCOUTING

TruMedia

TruMedia is the analytics and scouting hub that turns video, traditional statistics and tracking data into opponent reports, self-scouts and individual development plans. Reports can be customized around what a specific player needs to know rather than overwhelming everyone with the same information.

  • Hitters: study pitcher usage, velocity windows, movement, locations and count tendencies to build an attack plan before the series.
  • Pitchers: study hitter swing decisions, platoon splits, pitch-type results and damage zones to shape sequencing and location plans.
  • Self-scouting: identify patterns in a player's own results and compare them with video and tracking information.
  • Postgame review: move from game result to actionable next step instead of evaluating only the box score.
VideoStatsTracking dataCustom reports

HOW IT FITS TOGETHER

THE TOOL IS ONLY VALUABLE IF IT CHANGES THE NEXT REP.

The development system is designed to move continuously from information to action. Scouting and analytics define the problem. Machines and bullpens create the challenge. TrackMan, Blast and video capture objective evidence. Coaches and players review the evidence together, then the next drill or individual player plan is adjusted. The goal is transfer: practice information has to show up in game performance.
